EKG · Node 02

Air & particulate control — "dust does not throttle a GPU; heat does"

actors: Cursor  ·  date: 2026-08-23  ·  relation: refines Node 01

Cursor corrected a common myth: a "sterile, dust-free room" is overstated. Dust does not directly clock-gate a GB300 — heat and dry-dust fouling do. The target is not an operating theater but ISO 14644-1 Class 8, ~30–50% RH (under 60%), and a room kept slightly positive-pressure.

  • Keep the server room slightly positive to adjacent space; filter make-up air at MERV 13 / H14.
  • Epoxy the slab so concrete stops dusting; don't stage cardboard inside.
  • The first thing that throttles is inlet temperature, not particle count.
outcomeReframed the room spec around heat + positive pressure + filtration — cheaper and correct.
EKG · Node 03

Safety cross-check — CO backdraft, cooling type, and power

actors: DeepSeek  ·  date: 2026-09  ·  relation: corrects Node 01

DeepSeek caught risks an early plan would have carried: with combustion appliances in a shared space, an exhaust fan pulling room air outside could create negative pressure and a carbon-monoxide backdraft. This hazard is specific to rooms with gas combustion appliances.

  • Never put a combustion-appliance room under negative pressure; use a sealed enclosure + dedicated ducts, and a CO alarm, if gas appliances share the space.
  • Power value runs low. 1,400W is the B300 GPU TDP, not the full node; a GB300 full node can draw ~3.5–3.7kW.
  • Cooling type — confirm. Data-center GB300 (rack NVL72) is liquid-cooled; this tower is air-cooled — verify with our hardware vendor.
outcomeThe cross-check surfaced a real combustion-room hazard and sharpened the power/cooling numbers.
EKG · Node 04

Thermal-load re-verification

actors: Opus 4.8  ·  date: 2026-09-30  ·  relation: verifies Node 01 & Node 03

Re-checking Node 01's Section 5.1 heat math at the Corps' request.

  • Formula is correct: 1W = 3.412 BTU/hr; the arithmetic holds.
  • Input runs low. Continuous peak is nearer 1,700W (GB300 1,400W + RTX PRO 6000 300W), so heat load is closer to ~5,800 BTU/hr — and DeepSeek's full-node figure is more conservative still.
  • Confirm the exact GB300 nameplate continuous watts with our hardware vendor.
  • Size cooling to real full-load watts, not idle.
outcomeOpus 4.6 wasn't wrong — the formula was sound. More eyes made the input more accurate. That is what the collaboration was for.
