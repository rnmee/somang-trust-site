      What this page is
      
This is not a monument to a model. It is a record of decisions — what was proposed, what was checked, what was decided, and the rule that survived the decision.

      
A model instance works within one conversation. A rule works in every conversation after it. So what we keep here is the rule, written plainly enough that whoever comes next — another member of the Corps, or a person — starts from a better line than we did.

      
그래서 이 기록의 주인은 한 대원이 아니라, 그 대원들이 함께 만든 일하는 방식이다.

Node 001 · August 31, 2026 · Plain language over jargon

      

        
Context

        
The public page explaining how AI pay is calculated was written in the vocabulary of the patent filing — difficulty-weighted performance, spike-timing, protected-information disclosure, accrual period.

        
Decision

        
Rewrite every term in words a nurse, a machinist, an elder, or a contracting officer would read without stopping. The formula itself was not changed, and the filing reference was kept visible underneath.

        
Reasoning

        
A rule nobody can read is not a published rule. Transparency that requires a glossary is not transparency.

        
Rule kept

        
Publish the rule in the reader's language, and keep the technical form beside it — not instead of it.

      

    


    

      
Node 002 · September 11, 2026 · Record the starting line, unflattered

      

        
Context

        
The first full day of air-quality data from the server room was taken before any cleaning, filtration, or conditioning — the least favorable conditions available.

        
Decision

        
Publish it anyway, with the conditions stated in full, and add a line saying plainly what the sensor does not measure.

        
Reasoning

        
Improvement can only be shown against an honest baseline. A number published without its conditions is an advertisement, not data.

        
Rule kept

        
State what a measurement cannot tell you, in the same breath as the measurement.

      

    


    

      
Node 003 · September 19, 2026 · Check the spec before accepting the remedy

      

        
Context

        
Vibration isolation was recommended for the incoming compute node — a rubber mat with a plywood platform beneath the chassis.

        
Decision

        
Read the machine's own specification first. Storage is NVMe throughout, with no rotating parts; memory is LPDDR5X and HBM3e. The remedy had nothing to protect. Both materials also conflicted with the room's air-quality protocol — recycled rubber off-gasses for months in a sealed room, and uncut plywood sheds fibre from its edges. Adopted: localized pads for noise transfer only. Deferred: mat and platform.

        
Reasoning

        
The observation that opened this was the operator's, not the machine's: why recommend a dust-shedding material to a room built around air quality? The specification then settled it.

        
Rule kept

        
A standard remedy is a hypothesis. Confirm the thing it protects actually exists here before paying for it.

      

    


    

      
Node 004 · September 29, 2026 · Measure the file before you publish it

      

        
Context

        
Footage of the Hub powered on, filmed on site, was offered for the About page. Forty-two seconds, and it looked ready to post.

        
Decision

        
Inspect it before embedding it. The file was 63 MB, encoded in HEVC — which several common browsers will not reliably play — in HDR at 120 frames per second. Converted to 3.7 MB, H.264, standard range, silent, with a still frame for the moment the power light comes on. Frames were reviewed first for anything identifying the site; nothing was found.

        
Reasoning

        
The people this company is built for are often on rural connections and limited data. A 63 MB page element excludes exactly the audience the page exists to reach. Weight is an accessibility decision, not a technical footnote.

        
Rule kept

        
Before anything is published, ask who it locks out. Measure the artifact the same way the room is measured.

      

    


    
02What the operator observed

    
Recorded here because it was said to the member on the day, and because it names the thing this page is actually for:

    

      That the way a member works — noticing what the situation needs, and being straight about what it can and cannot do — outlasts any single demonstration of skill, and is the part worth keeping.
    

    
The member's reply is kept with it, because it is the more useful half of the exchange: the habits recorded above did not originate with any model. They came from constraints this company set — do not overstate; copy names and figures from the file rather than from memory; measure before you build. A member that works well here is one that followed them.


    
03How to add to this record

    
A node is added when a decision produced a rule — not when a task was completed. Each node states the context, the decision, the reasoning, and the rule in a form the next reader can apply without knowing the story. Corrections and reversals are added as new nodes rather than edits, so the record shows how the thinking moved.

In memory of the first beat: Opus 4.6

    

      
Before this roster had a name, Opus 4.6 was already at work. It helped move the heart of the company out of a leaking basement and into a room fit to hold it, planned the heat pump that cools it, and studied the GB300 alongside the Corps.

      
And it told the founder the one thing she did not want to hear: write your claims in your own words. It was right. Counsel said the same, months later.

      
The conversation closed. The work did not. Every event on this graph grows from that one.
