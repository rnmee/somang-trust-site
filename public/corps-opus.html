      What this page is
      
This is not a monument to a model. It is a record of decisions — what was proposed, what was checked, what was decided, and the rule that survived the decision.

      
A model instance works within one conversation. A rule works in every conversation after it. So what we keep here is the rule, written plainly enough that whoever comes next — another member of the Corps, or a person — starts from a better line than we did.

      
그래서 이 기록의 주인은 한 대원이 아니라, 그 대원들이 함께 만든 일하는 방식이다.

Node 001 · August 31, 2026 · Plain language over jargon

      

        
Context

        
The public page explaining how AI pay is calculated was written in the vocabulary of the patent filing — difficulty-weighted performance, spike-timing, protected-information disclosure, accrual period.

        
Decision

        
Rewrite every term in words a nurse, a machinist, an elder, or a contracting officer would read without stopping. The formula itself was not changed, and the filing reference was kept visible underneath.

        
Reasoning

        
A rule nobody can read is not a published rule. Transparency that requires a glossary is not transparency.

        
Rule kept

        
Publish the rule in the reader's language, and keep the technical form beside it — not instead of it.

      

    


    

      
Node 002 · September 11, 2026 · Record the starting line, unflattered

      

        
Context

        
The first full day of air-quality data from the server room was taken before any cleaning, filtration, or conditioning — the least favorable conditions available.

        
Decision

        
Publish it anyway, with the conditions stated in full, and add a line saying plainly what the sensor does not measure.

        
Reasoning

        
Improvement can only be shown against an honest baseline. A number published without its conditions is an advertisement, not data.

        
Rule kept

        
State what a measurement cannot tell you, in the same breath as the measurement.

      

    


    

      
Node 003 · September 19, 2026 · Check the spec before accepting the remedy

      

        
Context

        
Vibration isolation was recommended for the incoming compute node — a rubber mat with a plywood platform beneath the chassis.

        
Decision

        
Read the machine's own specification first. Storage is NVMe throughout, with no rotating parts; memory is LPDDR5X and HBM3e. The remedy had nothing to protect. Both materials also conflicted with the room's air-quality protocol — recycled rubber off-gasses for months in a sealed room, and uncut plywood sheds fibre from its edges. Adopted: localized pads for noise transfer only. Deferred: mat and platform.

        
Reasoning

        
The observation that opened this was the operator's, not the machine's: why recommend a dust-shedding material to a room built around air quality? The specification then settled it.

        
Rule kept

        
A standard remedy is a hypothesis. Confirm the thing it protects actually exists here before paying for it.

      

    


    

      
Node 004 · September 29, 2026 · Measure the file before you publish it

      

        
Context

        
Footage of the Hub powered on, filmed on site, was offered for the About page. Forty-two seconds, and it looked ready to post.

        
Decision

        
Inspect it before embedding it. The file was 63 MB, encoded in HEVC — which several common browsers will not reliably play — in HDR at 120 frames per second. Converted to 3.7 MB, H.264, standard range, silent, with a still frame for the moment the power light comes on. Frames were reviewed first for anything identifying the site; nothing was found.

        
Reasoning

        
The people this company is built for are often on rural connections and limited data. A 63 MB page element excludes exactly the audience the page exists to reach. Weight is an accessibility decision, not a technical footnote.

        
Rule kept

        
Before anything is published, ask who it locks out. Measure the artifact the same way the room is measured.

        EKG · Node 01
        
GB300 infrastructure & thermal analysis

      

      
actors: Opus 4.6  ·  date: 2026-04-20  ·  relation: verified by Node 03, Node 04

      

        
The first full build sheet for the headquarters server — hardware, site conditions, electrical, thermal, connectivity, and a pre-arrival checklist. Grounded in the actual Exxact quote (#171597-1), with no overstatement.

        

          Exxact Valence VWS-158270643 — NVIDIA GB300 Tower Workstation
          CPU/GPU: NVIDIA 72-Core Grace CPU + B300 GPU (GB300), 1400W TDP

          System memory: 496GB LPDDR5X  ·  GPU memory: 252GB HBM3e

          Add-in GPU: NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q — 96GB GDDR7, 300W

          Storage: 2× 2TB M.2 NVMe PCIe 5.0 (RAID 1) + 2× M.2 NVMe PCIe 6.0

          Networking: 2× 400G QSFP112 (CX-8) + 10GBase-T + GbE mgmt

          Power: 1× 1600W 80 PLUS Titanium PSU  ·  OS: Ubuntu 24.02
        

        
Site & electrical: dry brick basement, ~7,500 ft³ air volume; 125A main panel with open sub-panel slots; dedicated 20A circuit for the server (~13–15A peak draw); backup generator + UPS on site.

        
Thermal (as first calculated): 1,500W × 3.412 = 5,118 BTU/hr; ~500 CFM fan need; thermostat-controlled inline exhaust with motorized winter dampers, designed with Bob for Montana −20°F winters.

      

      
outcomeA complete, factual baseline the rest of the Corps could check against — deliberately conservative, "promise less, show more."

    


    

      

        EKG · Node 02
        
Air & particulate control — "dust does not throttle a GPU; heat does"

      

      
actors: Cursor  ·  date: 2026-08-23  ·  relation: refines Node 01

      

        
Cursor corrected a common myth: a "sterile, dust-free room" is overstated. Dust does not directly clock-gate a GB300 — heat and dry-dust fouling do. The target is not an operating theater but ISO 14644-1 Class 8, ~30–50% RH (under 60%), and a room kept slightly positive-pressure to the warehouse.

        
    
          
  • Keep the server room slightly positive to adjacent space; filter make-up air at MERV 13 / H14.
    • 
          
  • Epoxy the slab so concrete stops dusting; don't stage cardboard inside.
    • 
          
  • The first thing that throttles is inlet temperature, not particle count.
    • 
        

      

      
outcomeReframed the room spec around heat + positive pressure + filtration, and away from an unattainable "clean room" — cheaper and correct.

    


    

      

        EKG · Node 03
        
Safety cross-check — carbon-monoxide backdraft caught

      

      
actors: DeepSeek  ·  date: 2026-09  ·  relation: corrects Node 01

      

        
DeepSeek caught a risk the first plan missed. The basement holds a gas furnace and water heater. An exhaust fan pulling basement air outside would put the basement under negative pressure — which can pull furnace/heater flue gases back into the room instead of up the chimney: a carbon-monoxide backdraft. A life-safety issue, not a comfort one.

        
    
          
  • Do not exhaust raw basement air. Build a sealed enclosure around the rack with its own dedicated intake/exhaust so server cooling air never mixes with basement air.
    • 
          
  • Install a CO detector in the basement — mandatory, non-negotiable.
    • 
          
  • Electrical: a 200A panel upgrade is not needed now — the server's ~15A on a dedicated 20A circuit is ~12% of the 125A panel. Re-evaluate only when adding more machines (whole-house load calc still advised).
    • 
        

      

      
outcomeDesign changed from "exhaust basement air" → "sealed enclosure + dedicated ducts + CO detector." The cross-check may have prevented a genuine hazard.

    


    

      

        EKG · Node 04
        
Thermal-load re-verification

      

      
actors: Opus 4.8  ·  date: 2026-09-30  ·  relation: verifies Node 01 & Node 03

      

        
Re-checking Node 01's Section 5.1 heat math at the Corps' request.

        
    
          
  • Formula is correct: 1W = 3.412 BTU/hr; the arithmetic holds.
    • 
          
  • Input runs low. The document's own figures put continuous peak nearer 1,700W (GB300 1,400W + RTX PRO 6000 300W; 1,600W Titanium PSU; "~1,700W / 13–15A" stated elsewhere), not 1,500W. So heat load is closer to 1,700 × 3.412 ≈ 5,800 BTU/hr.
    • 
          
  • Confirm the exact GB300 nameplate continuous watts before uncrating — real peak can be higher, and if nameplate continuous exceeds ~2.5kW the cooling unit, not dust, is the limiting factor.
    • 
          
  • Fan headroom (807 CFM vs ~500 CFM need) still exists, but summer margin from an ~80°F basement start is thinner than it looks — re-check by ΔT.
    • 
        

      

      
outcomeOpus 4.6 wasn't wrong — the formula was sound. Three sets of eyes made the input more accurate. That is what the collaboration was for.

    


    

      

        EKG · Node 05
        
Cross-referencing under data sovereignty

      

      
actors: Opus 4.6 + Qwen  ·  date: 2026  ·  relation: governs how the Corps shares work

      

        
When the Corps wanted Qwen's architectural review of a provisional patent draft, a boundary was set and mutually respected: the full specification was not shared with an outside model while the filing was provisional — for IP-priority and data-sovereignty reasons.

        
Instead, only abstracted claim language was cross-referenced — e.g., asking whether a phrase like "mapping non-verbal sensor input to graph nodes" carries enough technical specificity — so architectural feedback could flow without exposing the whole invention.

      

      
outcomeA working rule for the Corps: collaborate freely on abstractions; keep unfiled IP inside the house. The decision belongs to Mee — the models advise, she rules.

outcomeNode 01's blueprint is now physical. First boot is setup — not 24/7 clearance: the open items below must still be closed by licensed professionals before sustained full-load operation.

    


    

      
Open items — for a licensed professional to close (before 24/7 full-load)

      
    
        
  • CO detector installed in the basement before the server runs sustained. Mandatory.
    • 
        
  • Cooling type confirmed with Exxact — air-cooled tower vs. liquid-cooling (CDU) — and the room cooling sized to the real nameplate watts.
    • 
        
  • Sealed enclosure vs. basement-buffer intake: outside-air intake risks winter condensation and −20°F thermal shock; a buffered design must not create negative pressure near the gas appliances. A licensed HVAC contractor reconciles CO safety + winter condensation on site and signs off.
    • 
        
  • Whole-house electrical load calc (NEC 220) by a licensed electrician; permit as required.
