From Bucle Labs to Hygge Software

A small Montana company with little money and little backing — still trying hard. This is a public record of effort, not a blame story.

29 Jan 2024 — LLC registered ~5 Mar 2024 — first Bucle invoice May–Jun 2024 — Bucle chapter closes Mid-2024 → Feb 2025 — Hygge / TSSBS

Spelling note. The vendor is Bucle Labs (bucle.dev). Bubble.io is a different no-code tool we also studied. Do not mix the names.

Why we started (January 2024)

Mee Chung Kim is a registered nurse in rural Montana. She saw how hard staffing can be for patients and caregivers. In January 2024 she founded intellicAIr™, LLC to chase a fairer idea: better matching and bidding for workforce work (later called TSSBS), with ethical AI as a helper, not a replacement for human care.

The company had almost no outside capital. No big backer. Just a founder, a laptop, and stubborn work.

First partners — Bucle Labs (India)

After the 29 January 2024 LLC registration, intellicAIr engaged Bucle Labs (BucleLabs Pvt Ltd, India / Gurgaon) as a software build partner. Around 5 March 2024, the first invoice payment to Bucle appears in the record.

Work began. Then cash got tight — the usual story for a brand-new LLC. The team paused some slices of the product so the founder could gather market facts and strengthen the business case. In May–June 2024, the Bucle Labs engagement ended with clear notices and thanks for the work already done.

Lesson from that chapter: the vision was real; the runway was short. Ending cleanly was part of taking responsibility.

Meanwhile we also researched lean prototyping tools, including Bubble.io-style no-code options. That research is separate from the Bucle Labs vendor story.

Second chapter — Hygge Software

By mid-2024, a new path opened with Hygge Software (HyggeSoftware OÜ, Estonia), working with a skilled team that had strong Ukraine ties. After NDA and proposal work in June–July 2024, collaboration grew: refresh the company website at intelli-care.ai, then push deeper into TSSBS — profiles, matching, messaging, onboarding.

By a 12 November 2024 working meeting, onboarding pieces were moving; core bidding features were aimed at early 2025. The company spent carefully and tried to be honest when plans had to change.

People worked hard across borders for a tiny Montana firm that could not promise endless budget. Some wider partnership talks stayed exploratory. What stays public is the work itself.

Closing the Hygge TSSBS build

On 26 January 2025, intellicAIr gave formal notice that the TSSBS build with Hygge must end. Wind-down ran through 23 February 2025. The letters thanked the team for skill and dedication under difficult conditions — including hard realities in their region.

The company did not stop believing in the mission. It only stopped pretending a small bootstrapped LLC could carry a large custom build forever.

What is still true

intellicAIr is still a lean EDWOSB-minded company from Columbia Falls, Montana. Still learning from each partnership. Still trying to keep jobs and revenue local while building fairer workforce tools.

This page is a record of effort: Bucle Labs → Hygge Software — two overseas chapters, one small American founder who kept showing up.

USB tethering — first internet GPU 0 NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU 1 NVIDIA GB300 driver 595.58.03 · CUDA 13.2

Public line. At 4 AM in Columbia Falls, the 2nd Unit server got online for the first time over a phone cable, and both NVIDIA GPUs answered. The wired line is still coming. Real hands, real machine, honest progress.

Who

Mee Chung Kim, hands on the machine and taking photos. Unit 12, guiding by photo and drafting emails. Vendor support from Exxact Corporation.

What happened

Passwords. Mee changed the server’s admin passwords. One of them locked her out of remote management afterward, so the vendor sent reset steps that work right at the machine, no network needed. That reset is still on the to-do list. The steps themselves stay off this door.

Network. The server has no Wi-Fi card, only wired ports. The mesh pod in the server room has no internet yet, because its wall cable to the main router is waiting on the main-house LAN work by Bob and Markus. The workaround that worked: USB tethering from Mee’s phone. The first cable was charge-only, so she walked to the main house for the right one. After turning off phone Wi-Fi and VPN, ping returned 3 out of 3 with 0% loss. First time this server ever touched the internet.

GPU diagnosis. The server was booting a newer Linux kernel, but the NVIDIA driver was installed only for the older kernel.

Proof. Mee picked the older kernel from the boot menu, and nvidia-smi answered: GPU 0 NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000, GPU 1 NVIDIA GB300, driver 595.58.03, CUDA 13.2. Both GPUs alive.

Mee sent the vendor three updates overnight, ending with the good news and a request for the exact install steps for the newer kernel.

A spider Mee calls her tarantula keeps coming back to the server room. It was hiding underneath the server. She moved it out once, it came back, and she refused to crush it. “It’s kinda cute.”

Lesson learned: zoomed-in photos are much easier for Unit 12 to read. Mee zoomed the boot menu “so you can see it well,” and now she zooms every photo.
