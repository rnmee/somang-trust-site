## One clue tied to Bokjiro (banking vs praxis)

**Do not design Bokjiro as banking / pipeline into “welfare recipients.”**

These pages sharpen that clue with Freire’s own vocabulary:

1. **Extension = deposit.** The associative field of “extension” is transmission → active Subject → content chosen by the transmitter → recipient → messianism → superiority/inferiority → mechanical transfer → **cultural invasion**. That is the banking shape: Subject deposits into Object.
2. **Assistencialism is the welfare trap.** Footnote language names *assistencialism* as symptom-level “assistance” with overtones of paternalism, dependency, and a hand-out approach—contrasted with *promocionalismo* (promoting vigorous self-capacity). Labeling people “welfare recipients” and piping answers into them is assistencialism with an AI wrapper.
3. **Integration ≠ adaptation.** Adaptation = person as object under external prescriptions (“adjusted”). Integration = adapt **plus** critical choice that transforms reality. Bokjiro that only “helps you comply” is adaptation theater.
4. **Dialogue, not communiqué.** Horizontal A↔B with matrix loving / humble / hopeful / trusting / critical. Vertical A-over-B issues communiqués. Anti-dialogue’s matrix is loveless, arrogant, hopeless, mistrustful, acritical.
5. **Praxis = action + reflection.** Technical “aid” that is not praxis is training-of-animals. Capacitation begins where people *are*, through problem-posing codification / de-coding—not slogans, not reading-control, not “forced to know.”
6. **“We think” establishes “I think.”** Knowledge is co-participation about a mediating object. Subject A must not turn Subject B into a depository for A’s thinking—or there is no communication.

**Design implication for Bokjiro (Montana conceptual MVP, attitude only):** co-investigation of blocked civic procedures (permits, taxes, pensions, utilities); mark information asymmetry; refuse classification-first UX; success = agency + completed next step, not compliance corrected into the system’s image.

## How Freire background / prison / exile folds in

The Introduction roots “Education as the Practice of Freedom” in adult literacy across Brazil **before** the military coup of **April 1, 1964**, which led to Freire’s **exile**. The essays keep the “naïveté” of earlier thought without disowning it—historicity over myth or consumption of “conscientização.” Closed-society analysis (colonial, slavocratic, reflex, anti-democratic; lack of participatory experience) is not abstract theory; it is the ground that made paternalism and vertical “listen and obey” consciousness durable. Prison and exile are power-sense: teacher/learner, extension agent/peasant, welfare clerk/recipient are body-level power relations. Later language (praxis as symbiosis of reflective action and critical theorizing; *Cultural Action for Freedom*) is earned under that pressure—not academic decoration.
